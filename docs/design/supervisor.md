# Design: Supervisor, Launch and Process Side (MVP PR 10a1)

Status: implemented (#46) in `crates/daemon/src/supervisor` and `crates/cli/src/agent_cmd.rs`. Scope is the launch and process part of the PR 10 row of `docs/mvp-design.md` §4 (cited as "mvp §N") that runs while the daemon owns its sessions, called **PR 10a1**: reservation, the `spawn`, `resume`, `stop`, and `close` commands and the `chaldea wait` CLI, the live process lifecycle and its death proof, natural exit, the hook token and binding facts of each launch, `extra` attribution, the environment, `GET /api/adapters` and `GET /api/threads/{thread_id}`, the host-OS mapping, the startup attention causes, the shutdown participant, and the restart reconcilers. The send and ack half (`send`, `ready`, `key`, `input_resolve`, `input_cancel`, the gate, the writer, ack attribution, the real `IntakeProbe`, input-side attention and cards) is **PR 10b**, designed against the interfaces of §2.11. Crash recovery (the restart probe of old processes, launch evidence, the owner's assertion, and mvp P-18) is **PR 10a2**, after 10b (§2.12).

**What 10a1 decides and leaves.** 10a1 owns the supervisor module of the daemon, the pure launch builder in `core`, small additions to `pty-runtime` (§1), and the CLI verbs above. It leaves to 10b everything that writes to a live PTY after `launch_started` and every ack decision; to 10a2 every rule that confirms the death of a launch the daemon could not prove dead itself; to PR 13 and PR 14 the hook configuration each CLI gets (which events, Claude's settings file, Codex's `-c hooks.*`), the payload decoders, and the verification of the argv rows on real binaries; to PR 16 agent tokens and worktree creation, and worktree removal, which is deferred; to PR 11 attach; to PR 18–19 the UI; to PR 21 the doctor's orphan finder, which is deferred (mvp §4 "Deferred").

**Owner decision (2026-11-25).** After a daemon crash, a check that covers only a launch's recorded identities does not unlock resume, close, or worktree release. The launch's death is confirmed only by the owner, with `chaldea stop <thread> --assume-dead --launch <id>` after reading the launch's evidence. PR 10a2 implements it (§2.12); 10a1 confirms nothing it did not prove.

**Commitments from earlier PRs this design keeps.**

1. Death proof is pty-runtime's. `death_confirmed` becomes true only from the first full-list proving report accepted by the op before the kill's submission + 12 s (from `Session::kill_by` via the one-shot, or as `CloseOutcome.kill` when `close_by` returns in time) in a stop or exit op, from the `CloseOutcome.kill.tree_dead` of the participant's or a late creation's close received by `close_end` (§2.9), or from a spawn error whose process provably never ran or whose cleanup is `Cleanup::Confirmed`. No census, no signal to a session or group the daemon does not own, no proof from a `Session` drop.
2. Every wait has a deadline through the daemon's helpers (`docs/design/deadlines.md`; `host::{act_before, observed_before}` on the synchronous side), with one stated exception (§2.10); an admitted write attempt's ticket is awaited to the writer's answer, as `run_write` awaits it (http-surface §2.8), the attempt's `not_after` bounding only the writer's start of the job.
3. Thread state moves only through explicit events: hooks, the backend's exit report, and the supervisor's own commands (invariant 1). Nothing reads PTY text.
4. Public types change only where §1 lists a new name. `StateInput`, `InputInput`, `reduce`, `step`, and `gate` keep their rules.

## 1. Goal and Dependencies

**Goal.** A CLI started by `chaldea spawn` or `chaldea resume` runs in a daemon-owned PTY with its own hook token and binding facts; each step of its life reaches the store as one explicit event in one job; every launch ends with `launch_exited`, its death confirmed exactly when an owned close or a spawn error proves it; every process the supervisor creates is counted until it is closed or reaped, and the instance lock is not released while the count is nonzero; a restart records every open launch, recovers every input, and revokes every launch token before the listener exists; and no thread is relaunched until its previous launch's death is confirmed.

**Depends on** PR 3 and PR 4 (backends, `Session`), PR 5 (`capabilities`, `check`), PR 8 (`Components`, reconcilers, participants, `spawn_locked`), PR 9 (`POST /hook`, binding, `buffer`).

## 2. Design

### 2.1 Rules

1. **One job per transition.** Reservation, start, stop request, exit, and close are each one write job that re-reads the rows it decides on and appends its events with the reducer's `status_changed`. Every job after the runner's intent goes through `supervisor::record` (§2.10).
2. **Launch operations outlive requests.** A command's effect hands its work to a `LaunchOp`, a supervisor-owned task, and awaits its result; the op runs to its end when the runner is aborted or the request goes away, and owns any `Session`, spawn, kill, or close result.
3. **One op lock per launch.** The live map is keyed by `LaunchId`; its entry, locked by its op, exists before the reservation commits; every use of the `Session` and every record of its outcome holds the lock (§2.11).
4. **Every owned process carries a tracker token.** A process creation first takes a `TrackToken` from `Tracker::admit`, which refuses once shutdown started; what it creates exists only inside a `TrackedSession` or `TrackedChild`, and its token is released only on a path that confirms the process and its handles released; every other end leaves it counted (§2.9).
5. **The full list on every kill.** Every `Session::kill_by` and `Session::close` gets the launch's full recorded-descendant list; a report's survivors are never passed back (pty-windows §2.5 "The caller's obligation").
6. **What a kill shows goes to the record or the log.** A kill that confirms records its method in the exit job; the survivors of one that cannot confirm are logged (§2.12).
7. **Startup causes for the current, started, unbound launch only**, decided in the job that raises them.

### 2.2 Commands

Registered through `CommandRegistry::register` in `Built::commands` (E1). The guard decides kind and action per name (`crates/core/src/auth.rs` `MVP_COMMANDS`, `grant`). Each handler's `prepare` parses the wire args (400 `bad_args` when they do not fit), mints ids and the hook token with `mint_token`, and builds an intent closure from data only; the handler object (holding `Arc<Supervisor>`) is used only by `effect` (http-surface §2.8).

**Target scope first.** The `resume`, `stop`, and `close` intents check the target thread against the token's grant (`may_act_on_thread`) before they read anything else of it; a refusal is `deny_for` (404 for a thread the token cannot read, identical to a missing one; http-surface §2.5 "Hiding"), with nothing written and no op started.

| Command | Args (wire types) | Intent (the runner's job) | Effect: `LaunchOp` | Success | Deadline |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `spawn` | `SpawnArgs { agent, cwd, prompt?, task? }` | object authorization; returns `Pending` | §2.3 "Spawn and resume" | `SpawnResult { thread_id, launch_id, input_id?, outcome: started \| spawn_failed }` | 30 s |
| `resume` | `ResumeArgs { thread_id, expected_launch_id, prompt? }` | target scope; returns `Pending` | §2.3 "Spawn and resume" (the reservation checks run in the op's reservation job) | `ResumeResult { launch_id, input_id?, outcome }` | 30 s |
| `stop` | `StopArgs { thread_id, expected_launch_id?, assume_dead? }` | target scope; with `assume_dead`, the owner's assertion (`docs/design/supervisor-recovery.md` §2.5); the current launch `L` (it must equal `expected_launch_id` when given, else `launch_changed`); `L` exited with death confirmed → `Completed(already_exited)`, result and receipt in this job, `confirmed_within_deadline` from `CommandCtx::job_start()` (the `StopResult` table below); exited unconfirmed → `death_unconfirmed`; else `Pending(L)` with `L`'s state and `CommandCtx::request_start()` | §2.3 "Stop" for `L` | `StopResult { launch_id, outcome: stopped \| already_exited \| asserted, death_confirmed, confirmed_within_deadline, exit_code, signal }` | 30 s |
| `close` | `CloseArgs { thread_id, expected_launch_id, stop?: bool }` | target scope; `thread_closed`; `launch_changed` (current ≠ `expected_launch_id`); then §2.3 "Close" | stop of a live `L`, then the closing job (with the nudge) | `CloseResult { thread_id, stop }` | 30 s |

`chaldea wait` is a CLI read loop, not a command (SD16).

**Caller.** An owner token spawns a top-level thread (`parent_thread_id: None`); an agent token (PR 16 mints them) spawns a child of its own thread (`parent_thread_id` = the token's thread, the `ChildrenOfSelf` grant of `auth.rs:144`). Both get `role: standard` (`--role rescue` is the deferred PR 20's; PR10a1-CL-34).

**Prompt admission** (C1; mvp §8 D5). Codex takes the prompt as its positional argument; Claude's becomes a `queued` input (`encoding: bracketed_paste`) that the reservation job creates after `launch_reserved` and the send side writes through the PTY once the gate opens (`docs/design/supervisor-send.md` §2.9 D3). In that order: longer than `MAX_PROMPT_BYTES` → 400 `prompt_too_large`; `check(agent, first-prompt encoding, Purpose::Send, text)` (`Positional` for Codex, `BracketedPaste` for Claude) → `Err` is 400 `prompt_rejected`; all before any receipt or event. `spawn` checks in `prepare`, its args naming the agent; `resume` checks in its intent, right after the target-scope check reads the thread's agent, and the refusal rolls the receipt back with the job. The CLI runs the same `check` first to print the reason, since the error body carries only the code. A Codex launch without a prompt is a manual launch (`LaunchReserved.manual`).

**Filesystem refusals** come from the op, after the receipt exists: a `cwd` that is not absolute, does not exist, or is not a directory is 400 `bad_args` with no reservation, token, event, or spawn (the PTY backends start the child in exactly that path, and the CLI sends an absolute one); the runner stores that reply, so a retry with the same `command_id` replays it even after the directory appears (a new request needs a new `command_id`). `prepare` reads no filesystem.

**Codes.** New `ErrorCode` variants, each classified in `handler_code` at exactly one status (E2):

| Code (new) | Status | When | The work can happen later? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `thread_closed` | 409 | resume or close on a closed thread (a closed thread's launch is confirmed dead, so its stop is `already_exited`) | no |
| `launch_changed` | 409 | `expected_launch_id` is not the thread's current launch; a stop or close `--stop` whose target launch stopped being current before its op acted | no |
| `launch_live` | 409 | resume or close (without `stop`) while the current launch has not exited | no |
| `death_unconfirmed` | 409 | resume, close, or stop while the current launch exited without confirmed death (resume also raises `resume_blocked` in the same job); the owner's assertion confirms it (§2.4) | no |
| `worktree_unavailable` | 409 | the launch's worktree is not `active` or `released` | no |
| `no_session` | 409 | resume of a thread with no bound `session_id` | no |
| `prompt_rejected` | 400 | `check` refused the positional prompt | no |
| `prompt_too_large` | 400 | the prompt exceeds `MAX_PROMPT_BYTES` (also 10b's admission code, C8) | no |
| `launch_busy` | 504 | a stop that could not take the op lock and re-read the launch within 10 s of its op's start (§2.3); nothing changed | no: the stop is abandoned, and a new `chaldea stop` may succeed |

Success types have no top-level `code` field (http-surface.md:544); a test asserts it per type (E2), so a process's exit code is `exit_code`.

**`death_unconfirmed` in the CLI.** The error body carries only the code, and the code is the same whether a crash or an owned close left the launch unconfirmed. `chaldea resume` and `close` pinned `expected_launch_id` from their read before the request (SD16); on this code they read `GET /api/threads/{thread_id}` once within the same cutoff, and only when its current launch is that pinned id and is exited with death unconfirmed do they print by its `exit_reason`: `daemon_restart` or `unknown_after_crash` → "The previous process of this thread could not be confirmed to have ended after the daemon restarted."; `stopped`, `exit`, or `spawn_failed` → "The previous process of this thread could not be confirmed to have ended when it stopped." In every other case (`chaldea stop`, which pins no launch; a read that fails or ends at the cutoff; another current launch) they print "The thread's last process could not be confirmed to have ended." Then, in every case, "To review what was checked, run `chaldea stop <thread> --assume-dead`; once you have made sure its processes ended, confirm with `--launch <id>`. Its history is kept, and new threads can be started.", with the thread's id for `<thread>`. The command exits 1. API clients read the same facts from the launch's `exit_reason` and `death_confirmed`.

**`StopResult` and `CloseResult`.** Every field is always present, `exit_code` and `signal` as `null` when unknown (`payload.rs:3-4`'s rule, with no `skip_serializing_if` and no `#[ts(optional)]`), and each is in the JSON Schema's `required` list. The 5 s of mvp P-3, counted from `RequestStart`, is reported here and bounds no work of the stop (§2.3). By branch:

| `outcome` | `death_confirmed` | `confirmed_within_deadline` (mvp P-3) | `exit_code`, `signal` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `stopped` | true exactly when there is the first full-list proving report accepted by the op before the kill's submission + 12 s (from `Session::kill_by` via the one-shot, or as `CloseOutcome.kill` when `close_by` returns in time), its `attempt` then `Signalled` or `AlreadyDead` (§2.3 Stop step 5) | `death_confirmed`, and that report's `KillReport.proved_at` is at or before `RequestStart` + 5 s | from `CloseOutcome.exit`; `null` when the waiter had not reported an exit |
| `already_exited`, decided by the intent | true (an exit without confirmed death is the `death_unconfirmed` refusal) | true exactly when `CommandCtx::job_start()` is at or before `RequestStart` + 5 s: the writer runs one job at a time, so the death the intent reads was confirmed before its job started; the row holds no proof time, so a slow intent can report `false` for a process that died in time (only the report and the CLI's exit 4 differ) | `null`: the launch's own `launch_exited` carries them |
| `already_exited`, decided by the op's re-read (the launch exited between the intent and the seal) | true | the same rule, with the clock read after that re-read returned | `null` |
| `already_exited` of a launch confirmed by `owner_asserted`, from the intent or the re-read | true | false: an assertion is not a stop's proof | `null` |
| `asserted` (only to `assume_dead`; `docs/design/supervisor-recovery.md` §2.5) | true | false | `null` |

`CloseResult.stop` is the `StopResult` of the stop a `close --stop` ran (§2.3 "Close"): `stopped`, or `already_exited` when the launch exited between the close's intent and the stop's seal; it is `null` without `--stop` and when the close's intent found the launch exited, since no stop ran. `chaldea stop` exits 0 when `confirmed_within_deadline` is true. When `outcome` is `asserted`, it prints "The owner confirmed that launch <id> has ended." and exits 0. When `death_confirmed` is true and `confirmed_within_deadline` false, it prints one line, "The process ended, but its end was not confirmed within 5 s of the stop request the daemon accepted." (for `already_exited`: "The process had already ended, but this stop did not confirm its end within 5 s (its end may have been confirmed by the owner's assertion).", since the result does not say which), and exits 4 (`ExitCode::Timeout`, `lifecycle/mod.rs:243-251`, the code `chaldea wait` uses for a passed cutoff). When `death_confirmed` is false, it prints the general message above and exits 1 (`ExitCode::Failed`). `chaldea close --stop` reads `CloseResult.stop` the same way once the thread is closed; an inner stop that did not confirm death ends the close as the `death_unconfirmed` refusal.

### 2.3 Launch Lifecycle

**Spawn and resume** (the `LaunchOp`; `L` is the launch id minted in `prepare`):

1. *Inputs.* `cwd` (spawn) must be an absolute path to a directory, else 400 `bad_args` (§2.2). Resolve the program (§2.7); a missing one is kept as `None`. Spawn only, the workspace probe: `supervisor::workspace::common_dir(cwd, now + 5 s)` runs `git -C <cwd> rev-parse --path-format=absolute --git-common-dir` (stdin and stderr null, stdout piped; `CREATE_NO_WINDOW` on Windows through `host::no_console_window`; without git's own variables, those `git rev-parse --local-env-vars` lists, such as `GIT_DIR`, so a daemon started inside a repository's hooks or with them set discovers from `cwd` alone; and without `GIT_DISCOVERY_ACROSS_FILESYSTEM`, which can let discovery reach a parent repository on another filesystem; `GIT_CEILING_DIRECTORIES` stays, since it only makes the canonical cwd the id; the path is git's output without the LF git adds, and on Windows its CR) on a blocking task, which the op awaits with `settle_before` until the deadline + 1 s. One git child exists at a time. The supervisor's `GitSlot` holds, under one short mutex, a `busy` flag and at most one `left` child awaiting its reap; the probe takes a `GitLease` (sets `busy`) only when neither is set, else it uses the canonical cwd at once, never waiting. Holding the lease, it creates the child with `TrackedChild::spawn` into the lease and polls `TrackedChild::try_reap` through `host::observed_before` every 10 ms; at each poll where `Tracker::accepting()` is false (shutdown), it kills the child through its own handle (`TrackedChild::kill`; `rev-parse` only reads) and keeps polling. `TrackedChild::spawn` takes the spawn lock with `spawn_locked_before` by the deadline; holding it, it takes a token from `Tracker::admit`, which holds the tracker's mutex only for that check and count, so a probe that got the spawn lock after admission closed gets no token and creates no child; then it runs the OS spawn outside the tracker's mutex through `host::act_before` with the probe's deadline, the last check before the OS call (`host/mod.rs:568-573`), so a probe stalled after its admit creates no child past the deadline and releases its token. The token and the lease's `busy` flag, set before the spawn and kept until the child is reaped or handed to `left`, mark the creation in progress: shutdown closes admission at once and never waits for a spawn, and an OS spawn that has not returned by `end` holds its token, so the participant answers `LeftRunning` (§2.9). Exit 0 → the printed path (read after exit; one line, far below a pipe buffer). The slot busy or a child left, the spawn lock not taken, no token (admission closed), a nonzero exit, or no exit by the deadline → the canonical cwd, and the daemon log names which. At the deadline an unexited child is killed the same way. The lease's drop, on every path including a panic, kills an unreaped child through its own handle, moves it with its token into `left`, clears `busy` in the same critical section, and wakes `GitSlot.changed` (a `Notify`); a tick then reaps it (§2.6). `launch::workspace_id(path)` = `ws-` + the first 16 hex digits of SHA-256 of the path's UTF-8 bytes (mvp §2.1:103). Then, for a Claude spawn or resume (the resume's agent read from its thread), the version probe (C1 D11, `supervisor::version`): `<program> --version` as a `TrackedChild` the same way, in its own process tree (`host::own_tree`, `host::adopt_tree`: a new process group on Unix; on Windows created suspended, assigned to a new kill-on-close job, then resumed), with stdin and stderr null and stdout an anonymous temporary file (no pipe, so no read blocks and a descendant holding stdout delays nothing), polled every 10 ms through `host::observed_before` under one deadline of 5 s (`VERSION_PROBE`) on the supervisor's clock that covers the exit, the read, and the op's `settle_before`. Its exit is observed without reaping it on Unix (`waitid` with `WNOWAIT`), so the group id is still the child's when the whole tree's kill is requested; then the child is reaped and the file read at offsets (a descendant outside the tree, or a write in flight, can still change what is read: C1 §3). Exit 0 with at most 4 KiB of output (`MAX_OUTPUT`) → its first token, else (no program, no token, no tree, a nonzero exit, more output, the deadline) `null` at once, the tree and the child killed and, if unreaped, the child kept with its token in `VersionLeft` for the tick. The launch proceeds either way.
2. *Slot.* Insert the `LiveLaunch` for `L` with its op lock taken by this op.
3. *Reservation job.* Resume first re-reads the thread and its current launch and refuses, in this order: `thread_closed`; `launch_changed` (current ≠ `expected_launch_id`); `launch_live` (no `exit_reason`); `death_unconfirmed`, raising `resume_blocked` when absent (mvp §2.9 "Daemon restart"); `worktree_unavailable` (the previous launch's `worktree_id`, if any: only `active` and `released` pass; mvp CL-18); `no_session`. A refusal commits only the raise, removes the slot, and is the op's result. Then, in the same job: spawn's `thread_registered`; `launch_reserved` (`expected_previous`, `argv_digest` of the planned argv, `manual`, and for resume the previous launch's `worktree_id`, so `launches_using_worktree` returns the new launch); `StateInput::Reserved`; the positional `input_created { initial_state: sending, encoding: positional, origin: cli }`; and `token_insert` of the hook token. A rollback removes the slot.
4. *Spawn.* With no program, no token is taken and step 6's `NeverStarted` branch follows. Otherwise take a token from `Tracker::admit` and set the launch's `creation_started`, both before any process exists; then `Session::spawn(runtime, &spec, config)` on `spawn_blocking`, with the token moved into the blocking closure: `Ok` leaves the closure as `TrackedSession::new(session, token)`; an `Err` whose `cleanup` is `Cleanup::Confirmed` releases the token; an `Err` with `Cleanup::Unresolved`, and a panic inside `Session::spawn`, leave it counted (§2.9). The op awaits the closure to its result (§2.10). No token (admission closed) → the `NeverStarted` branch of step 6, with no process created. `config.on_event` forwards `SessionEvent::Exited` and `SessionEvent::WaitFailed` to the launch's exit op without blocking.
5. *Publication.* On `Ok(tracked)`: take `observer = session.observer()` and `reference = observer.last_started(ClassSet::ALL)`; then `Tracker::while_accepting` runs, under the tracker's mutex and only while admission is open, the publication itself: the closure borrows the op's `Option<TrackedSession>` and takes the session out of it only when it runs, putting it into the slot through the op lock this op holds and setting `Published { observer, reference }`, so a refused publication leaves the session with the op. `Tracker::close_admission` takes the same mutex, so a publication either completes before admission closes or does not happen; in the second case the op neither publishes nor starts the session and closes it itself (§2.9 "Late creations"). A write with a `seq` above `reference` is a write after the positional prompt's anchor (A35's anchor; 10b decides what it means for an ack).
6. *Start job*, by outcome:
   - `Ok(tracked)` → `launch_started { pid, start_time, pgid_or_job, cli_version }` from `Session::identity` and step 1's version (also on the launch row, where hook `raw_unknown` events take it from; `null` for Codex), `StateInput::Spawned`, the positional input `sending → sent` (`InputInput::WriteCompleted`, `sent_after_seq` = this `launch_started`'s seq; mvp §2.3:186), and for an upgraded Codex launch the `startup_stall` raise (§2.6). After the commit the op sets `CommittedStart { seq, identity, at }` (`at` read after the commit, on the supervisor's clock) and releases the op lock.
   - `Err(SpawnError::NeverStarted { .. })`, no token, or no program → `launch_exited { reason: spawn_failed, death_confirmed: true }` (no process ran and any process created was reaped, `backend.rs:186-193`), the positional input `sending → rejected(spawn_failed)`, and the exit steps of §2.11.
   - `Err(SpawnError::StartedThenFailed { cleanup, identity, .. })` → `launch_exited { reason: spawn_failed, death_confirmed: cleanup == Cleanup::Confirmed, failed_start }`, where `failed_start` is the returned identity when one was returned and the cleanup is unresolved, else absent: `pid`, and `start_time` and `pgid_or_job` each absent when the backend returned it empty (a Windows failure path can, `windows/mod.rs:933-937`), so such a record never identifies a process by its pid alone; the positional input `sending → unconfirmed` (the CLI may have read its argv) with `input_unconfirmed`; and the exit steps. The launch has no `launch_started`: that event stays the start transition. An unresolved cleanup's kill and close reports are logged (§2.12).

Fresh and resumed launches reserve in the op rather than in the intent because the slot must exist before the reservation is visible (rule 3) and spawn needs the workspace probe first (SD11).

**Stop** (the `LaunchOp` for `L`; `t0` is the op's start). mvp P-3 measures from `chaldea stop`, so the stop reports against `RequestStart` + 5 s, where `RequestStart` is the first head byte of the HTTP attempt the runner accepted, before the guard authenticates; time the CLI spent on refused attempts before it (a 408 before the intent, then a retry with the same `command_id`) is outside it (`http/ctx.rs:22-25`): `dispatch` copies it into `Admitted.start`, and the intent hands `CommandCtx::request_start()` to the op. That instant bounds no work. The lock wait and the re-read end at `t0` + 10 s, the stop's `StopRequested` write is one attempt whose ordinary `not_after`, `COMPLETION_ATTEMPT`, bounds only when the writer may start it, and the kill task has an ordinary cleanup budget fixed when it starts. No bound holds against a blocking task, a store, or a runtime paused past it, so every outcome says what happened: before the seal, 504 `launch_busy` with nothing changed; after it, `death_confirmed` and `confirmed_within_deadline` (§2.2), where only a missing proof leaves the launch unconfirmed (L-9). The server and the supervisor read one `MonotonicClock` (`SystemMonotonic`; a test passes one controlled clock to both), and every instant the supervisor hands to pty-runtime or compares with a report's `proved_at` goes through `MonotonicClock::real` (`http/mod.rs:72-79`).

1. *Lock.* The op takes the op lock of `L` with `deadline::settle_before(…, t0 + 10 s)` and `in_time()`; for a launch the intent read `starting`, this waits for the spawn outcome (X5, L-7). `Elapsed` or `Late` (a `Late` guard is dropped at once) → 504 `launch_busy`: nothing changed, and nothing stops the launch later. On a running launch the lock's other holders are an exit op, after which the stop reads `L` exited, and 10b's `with_session` calls, which do not block. The slot exists while `L` is not exited, since it precedes the reservation commit; a missing slot means `L` exited, and the op reads it.
2. *Re-read.* A read transaction of `L` on a blocking task, its pool wait and its result awaited with `settle_before` until `t0` + 10 s, as `read_until` bounds a handler's read (`ctx.rs:40-52`); a read not returned by then → 504 `launch_busy`, nothing changed (the read ends on its own). Not current (a resume replaced it) → `launch_changed`; exited with death confirmed (it exited after the intent) → `already_exited`, its `confirmed_within_deadline` true exactly when this read returned by `RequestStart` + 5 s (§2.2); exited unconfirmed → `death_unconfirmed`.
3. *Seal, submit `StopRequested`, then send the kill.* In one step under the op lock, with no await inside: `Session::seal()` (its records appended to the launch's `settled`, §2.11), the launch's in-memory `stop_requested` set, and the `TrackedSession` taken out of the slot (its token moves with it). The seal is the irreversible stop boundary (SD18, §2.11 "Stop before write"): from it the stop goes on whatever the store does, and this op owns the launch's exit record ("Natural exit" below). Still holding the op lock, the op writes `StateInput::StopRequested` itself: one `supervisor::record` job through `write_with` with `not_after = now + COMPLETION_ATTEMPT` (5 s, `commands.rs:46`), not retried. That `not_after` bounds only the writer's start of the job, checked again under the write lock (`writer.rs:361-363`), not its commit, so the kill does not wait for the answer. The op's `on_submit` for this write calls `spawn_blocking` for the kill task and reads the clock for that submission, which starts the cutoff of step 5, and hands the instant and the task's handle to the op; `write_with` runs it synchronously right after `Store::submit` returns, whatever the submission's outcome, before it examines that outcome, so before any await and before its fatal sink can run. `Store::submit` waits neither for queue space nor for the writer; it takes only the `shared.sender` mutex for its admission check (`store/mod.rs:461-490`), so the kill follows that short synchronous section. The op then keeps awaiting the write to the writer's answer, as for every admitted attempt, concurrently with step 5's waits, so a `SubmitError::Failed` at once, a `CommitUnknown`, a `WriterGone`, or a `Failed` takes `write_with`'s ordinary fatal path (`ctx.rs:206-210`, `ctx.rs:222-232`), which starts the daemon's shutdown, only after the kill task was submitted; that task runs unless the daemon process exits before the blocking pool starts it (L-8). When `StopRequested` did not commit, the exit job applies it from the state it reads (step 5).
4. *The kill task.* At its start `s` it fixes its cleanup budget, `CloseBounds::default()` (`limits.rs:61-78`): kills until `kill_end = s + 5 s`, then the close's other steps, 6 s, so the task ends by `s` + 11 s. It calls `Session::kill_by(&extra, lock_by, kill_end)` with `lock_by = min(now + KILL_LOCK_WAIT, kill_end)` (`KILL_LOCK_WAIT` = 250 ms). The backend waits for a held kill-state lock only until `lock_by`, and a lock that was held at the first try and obtained only at or after `lock_by` counts as not taken: a busy report with no signal (`unix/kill.rs:557-573`, `windows/kill.rs:338-346`). Otherwise it signals and proves until `kill_end`, and a call made at or after its deadline still makes no OS call, as pty-unix and pty-windows §2.5 step 1 state (`unix/kill.rs:589-617`, `windows/kill.rs:321-337`). Each report gains two fields. `KillReport.attempt` is `Signalled`; `AlreadyDead`, proven dead before any signal; or `NotAttempted`: busy, expired, or on macOS ownership lost before any signal. `KillReport.proved_at` is the backend's clock read right after the read that proved the tree dead, `None` unless `tree_dead`. `tree_dead: true` comes only with `Signalled` or `AlreadyDead`. The proof boundary is the backends' existing one: a proof from the last OS observation started before `kill_end` is accepted even when that observation completes after it (`unix/kill.rs:385-399`, `windows/kill.rs:397-407`), so `proved_at` can fall slightly after `kill_end`, and `confirmed_within_deadline` is judged by that actual `proved_at`. Only a busy report (`KillReport::is_busy`: `unix_busy`, `windows_busy`, FakePty's `fake_busy`) is retried, with the same full list, each call starting at least `KILL_LOCK_WAIT` after the previous one, while `kill_end` has not passed; any other report ends the kills. Every call gets the full list, so the first report with `tree_dead: true` covers the full target set: the task hands it to the op at once on a one-shot channel, whatever the rest of the close does. Then `TrackedSession::close_by(&extra, kill_end, CloseBounds::default())`: its kill step gets `kill_end` as an absolute instant and reuses the last report when that proved the tree dead for the same full list (`Session::close` reuses one only when `extra` is empty, `session.rs:843-848`; `close_by` also reuses one for an identical non-empty list), so `CloseOutcome.kill` is the report that obtained the proof, with its own `proved_at`, whether the close's kill step or an earlier call proved it; the drain and join after it do not change that instant. A proof that only the close's kill step obtains reaches the op when `close_by` returns, with no callback from inside the close (L-9). A task paused past `kill_end` before a call makes that call expired: it sends nothing and reports `NotAttempted`, and the launch ends unconfirmed (L-9). The task logs its start, `kill_end`, and each call's `lock_by`, attempt, method, and `proved_at`. The token follows §2.9's release rule: a close that did not prove the tree dead leaves it counted.
5. *Exit.* The op waits concurrently for the one-shot and the task's join, each through `settle_before` with one absolute cutoff, the kill's submission + 12 s (the task's 11 s and 1 s for the pool to start it). A value counts only when received before the cutoff: `settle_before` classifies a value by one clock reading taken after it was received, `Late` at or after the deadline (`deadline.rs:33-49`). Only after both waits are classified (`InTime`, `Late`, or `Elapsed`) and the `StopRequested` write has its answer does the op run the exit job of §2.11: `StateInput::StopRequested` when the recorded state is still `running` (the op's write did not commit); `launch_exited { reason: stopped }` with `code` and `signal` from `CloseOutcome.exit` (null when the waiter had not recorded an exit by the join, or the task had not returned); `death_confirmed` true exactly when there is the first full-list proving report accepted by the op before the kill's submission + 12 s (from `Session::kill_by` via the one-shot, or as `CloseOutcome.kill` when `close_by` returns in time), its `attempt` then `Signalled` or `AlreadyDead`; `ExitFacts.sealed` = the launch's `settled`; then `launch_death_confirmed { method }` with that report's method in the same job. `StopResult.confirmed_within_deadline` is `death_confirmed` and that report's `proved_at` at or before `real(RequestStart + 5 s)`; a proof obtained later and accepted before the cutoff confirms death and is reported late. A close failure or panic after that report was accepted does not revoke it: it keeps the token counted as a cleanup failure (§2.9). A task not returned by the cutoff runs on with its token, which only §2.9's release rule frees once its close ends, and logs its result; without an accepted report, the launch records `death_confirmed: false`. A kill or close that ended with `tree_dead: false` has its survivors logged.

**The reply deadline.** The command's 30 s is the HTTP reply deadline, not a budget of the stop. Inside it run authentication, the intent, the lock wait and re-read (bounded by `t0` + 10 s), the `StopRequested` write (one attempt, started by the writer only before its `COMPLETION_ATTEMPT` `not_after`), the kill task (observed until the kill's submission + 12 s), the exit job, the runner's receipt completion, and for `close --stop` the closing job. Each bound limits its own stage only, so a terminal reply within 30 s is not guaranteed; the HTTP reply follows its own `reply_by`. The runner answers from its progress at `reply_by = deadline + write_reply_grace` (`commands.rs:526`, `commands.rs:582-587`): a result read before `reply_by` is the reply; `IntentCommitted` → 409 `in_progress`; `Submitted`, the intent's commit not yet seen, and a `Done` first read at or after `reply_by` → 503 `outcome_unknown`. The op runs to its end either way. A retry with the same `command_id` answers `in_progress` until the receipt's completion commits, and the stored result after it.

**Natural exit.** The exit op receives `SessionEvent::Exited(exit)` or `SessionEvent::WaitFailed`. It reads the launch's `stop_requested` before it waits for the op lock and again after each round: once it is set, a stop has sealed the session and owns the exit record, so the exit op returns without recording or reporting, even while that stop holds the lock awaiting its `StopRequested` write (Stop step 3), whose kill can itself make the child exit. Otherwise it takes the op lock in 10 s rounds, re-reading the launch after each (a stop holding the lock records the exit itself); every holder's work is bounded (§2.10), so after 60 s it reports `StoreFatal::LaunchUnrecorded`, unless shutdown started, when it ends without a report and the next start records the launch (§2.5). Holding the lock, it returns without recording when `L` has an `exit_reason`, when `stop_requested` is set, or when the slot is empty: the op that sealed or took the session owns the exit record (a stop from its seal, the participant from its take-out), and when that op ended without recording (`Unrecorded`, or shutdown), the row stays open and the next start records it (§2.5). Else it seals the session (records appended to `settled`), takes it out of the slot, and starts Stop's kill task (its budget fixed at its start), whose `close_by` is the session's explicit close. It waits for the one-shot and the task's join as Stop step 5 does, each through `settle_before` against the kill's submission + 12 s, and once both are classified records `reason: exit` with `code` and `signal` from the callback's `ChildExit` (null after `WaitFailed`) and `ExitFacts.sealed` = `settled`, without waiting for a close still running at the cutoff, which runs on with its token (§2.9). `WaitFailed` means the waiter could not report an exit (pty-runtime §2.2; on macOS after ownership was lost, after another wait error, or after a waiter panic, `unix/child.rs:183-200`; on Windows a waiter error independent of the job proof, `windows/child.rs:69-85`); it decides nothing about death, which the acceptance rule of Commitment #1 decides in every case, as for a stop (A25).

**Close.** The intent, with or without `stop`: target scope, `thread_closed`, `launch_changed` (current ≠ `expected_launch_id`). Then without `stop`, or with `stop` and an exited launch: `launch_live`, `death_unconfirmed`, else `thread_closed { by }` and `token_revoke(TokenSelector::Thread(t))` in the same job. With `stop` and a live launch: `Pending(L)`; the op runs Stop for `L`, reported against the close's `RequestStart`, then the closing job with the same checks (a resume that reserved a newer launch meanwhile gives `launch_changed`; an unconfirmed stop gives `death_unconfirmed`); `CloseResult.stop` carries the stop's `StopResult` (§2.2). Worktree hand-over (mvp §2.6) is PR 16's, added to the closing job; `after_close` (PR 20) is deferred.

**Requests and ops.** The op's result is the effect's result. `CommandEffect::run` resolves to `EffectOutcome`:

- `Done(CommandResult)`: the runner completes the receipt and replies, as today.
- `Unrecorded`: a job of the op could not be recorded within `supervisor::record`'s budget while the daemon runs. The runner does not complete the receipt (it stays `in_progress` and becomes `unknown_after_crash` at the next start), replies 503 `outcome_unknown` if the request is still waiting, and reports `StoreFatal::ReceiptUncompleted` (`commands.rs:681-697`). A budget longer than the request's deadline means the client has usually received 409 `in_progress` first.
- `ShuttingDown`: the op stopped because admission closed or the store refused a write (`Closed`) while shutdown runs. On the wire it is the same as `Unrecorded`: the receipt stays `in_progress`, and a waiting request gets 503 `outcome_unknown`, because the intent and possibly earlier jobs of the op (a `close --stop`'s stop) have committed. The runner reports nothing, so a graceful stop does not end as a fatal. `shutting_down` stays the answer for requests refused before their intent (http-surface §2.9).

A handler cannot reply 503 itself (`result_allowed`, `commands.rs:152-176`), and a stored 500 would claim an outcome nobody knows.

### 2.4 Death Proof

**While the daemon owns the session**, a stop or exit op confirms death only from the first full-list proving report accepted by the op before the kill's submission + 12 s (from `Session::kill_by` via the one-shot, or as `CloseOutcome.kill` when `close_by` returns in time) (§2.3). The `Session` leaves the slot only into that close; a `TrackedSession` dropped elsewhere (a panic) closes its `Session` with `&[]` and is never recorded as a proof (A27).

**`extra` and its source** (B1, A3, A26). The supervisor passes, on every `kill` and `close`, every identity it can prove belongs to the launch: its own spawn records and containment it can show (hook-ingest D10). In the MVP no such identity exists outside what the backend already covers: job members (Windows) and session and group members observed while owned (macOS) are the backend's, the daemon creates no other process per launch, and PR 9 records none (hook-ingest D10). So the full list is the empty list on every call; a descendant that left the session before any observation is PR3-P-1's stated limit. A later producer records identities on the `LiveLaunch` and they are passed from then on.

**Unconfirmed launches.** A launch exits with `death_confirmed: false` when its owned kills and close could not prove death, when a `StartedThenFailed` cleanup is unresolved, or at a restart (`daemon_restart`, `unknown_after_crash`; §2.5). Until the owner confirms it, `stop` answers `death_unconfirmed`, `resume` and `close` refuse with it (resume raising `resume_blocked`), and the launch's worktree stays in use. New threads spawn as usual, and history is intact. The owner confirms such a launch with `chaldea stop <thread> --assume-dead --launch <id>`, after reading what was checked (`docs/design/supervisor-recovery.md` §2.5); no check confirms it.

**A29 on Windows.** A crash between the suspended `CreateProcessW` and `AssignProcessToJobObject` leaves a reservation without identity and a suspended child outside the launch's job (pty-windows §2.2 "Crash window"). 10a1 records the launch `unknown_after_crash` (§2.5) and keeps pty-windows' suspended fallback unchanged (SD2); what PR 21's finder (deferred) receives is 10a2's.

### 2.5 Restart

**Reconcilers** (E3; daemon-lifecycle §2.14: each its own job, at step 9, before the listener exists), returned by `SupervisorComponents::reconcilers` in this order:

1. `receipts_mark_unknown_after_crash` (PR 8, unchanged).
2. `supervisor_launches`, one job:
   1. For each `open_launches()` row with `exit_reason` null, in `(thread_id, reserved_seq)` order: `launch_exited { reason: daemon_restart` if `pid` is recorded, else `unknown_after_crash`; `code`, `signal` null; `death_confirmed: false }` with `StateInput::Exited`, then the exit steps of §2.11 with `restart: true`.
   2. Every input of any thread in `queued`, `sending`, or `sent` (`ReadTx::inputs_in_states`): `queued → cancelled`, `sending`/`sent → unconfirmed` through `InputInput::DaemonRestarted`, raising `input_unconfirmed`. Inputs already `unconfirmed` keep their state and attention (`step(Unconfirmed, DaemonRestarted)` is invalid).
   3. `token_revoke(TokenSelector::LaunchScoped)`: every token bound to a launch, exited ones included, since none of them is current and live after a restart (hook-ingest §2.8: revocation at restart ends a hook token). Owner tokens have no launch and stay.
   A second restart writes nothing: `open_launches()` still returns the launches exited with death unconfirmed (`readers.rs:775-785`), but none has `exit_reason` null, and no input is in those states and no launch token unrevoked.
3. `supervisor_buffers`, one job: `buffer::resolve_unbindable` for each `buffered_launches()` (A22; every launch is exited by now). It keeps daemon-lifecycle's order "launches, then buffered deliveries" and covers buffers left by launches that exited in an earlier run.

`ExitStep`s and `TickStep`s are in place before step 9 (§2.11 "Where 10b plugs in"), so 10b's steps run at restart too. No nudge is needed at restart: no socket exists before the listener. The positional input of a launch that crashed before `launch_started` ends `unconfirmed` through step 2.2 (A21). 10a1 sends no signal to old processes at startup and creates none (SD3, L-2): the daemon crate's dependencies leave it two ways to signal a process, a `Session` from its `PtyRuntime` and a `Child` it created (`crates/daemon/Cargo.toml`: no `libc`, `rustix`, or `windows-sys`, and tokio without `process`), and a restart has neither for an old launch.

### 2.6 Startup Causes and the Tick

One supervisor task ticks every second; its timer is the new exception `supervisor::tick_timer` (`sleep_until`). `Supervisor::tick(now)` is the seam tests drive with an injected instant. Each tick kills (again, harmlessly) and calls `TrackedChild::try_reap` on the `GitSlot`'s `left` child, if any (§2.3 step 1), takes a candidate list from the live map, and then runs, per candidate, a job that re-validates everything; registered `TickStep`s (10b's gate timeout and ack window) run after 10a1's:

- **`session_start_timeout`** (D4, A32): a candidate is a non-manual launch with a `CommittedStart` and `now ≥ CommittedStart.at + 20 s` (mvp §2.2:142) on the supervisor's monotonic clock; publication for observation (§2.3 step 5) is not a start. The job raises `AttentionCause::SessionStartTimeout` only if the launch is the thread's current launch, its row has `launch_started`'s identity, the thread's process state is `running` or `stopping`, it has no `session_id` (unbound), and the cause is absent. The condition is "unbound", not "no `SessionStart` seen": a `SessionStart` whose walk ends below the launch is `foreign_session` and leaves the launch unbound, and hook-ingest §2.5 (line 268) assigns reporting it to this cause (Quint change in §7).
- **`buffer::expire`** for each live, unbound launch with buffered deliveries (A22); its age is decided by store stamps as PR 9 designed (`buffer.rs:92-106`).

A tick job that cannot be recorded within its budget is skipped; the next tick decides again from the store. **`startup_stall`** (A32) is raised in the start job for a Codex launch whose plan says `upgraded`; `upgraded` is false until PR 14 supplies the CLI-version comparison (mvp §2.7 "Hook trust"). Both causes are cleared by the reducer on `StateInput::SessionBound` and `StateInput::Exited`. Hole c (formal-specs §3) is closed by the job-side guards: nothing raises a startup cause for an exited, unstarted, or non-current launch.

### 2.9 Components, Ownership, Shutdown, and Fatal Outcomes

**Components.** `chaldea daemon run` builds `SupervisorComponents::new(host_os, SupervisorConfig::default())` instead of `MvpComponents` (`daemon_cmd.rs:136-139`; tests pass their own through `Seams.components`). Its `reconcilers` are §2.5's; its `build` creates the `Supervisor`, the four handlers, the participant, and the mounts `Mounts::mvp_with(hook_seams, extra_reads)`, where `hook_seams` is `HookSeams::mvp()` in 10a1 and `extra_reads` mounts `GET /api/adapters`; its `attach` takes the `RevocationNudge` and starts the tick.

**Ownership as a type** (SD10). `supervisor::tracker` holds, under one mutex, the admission flag and the count of tokens not yet released:

```rust
// crates/daemon/src/supervisor/tracker.rs (new)
pub(crate) struct Tracker { /* Mutex<{ accepting: bool, count: usize }>, a Notify woken when count reaches 0 */ }
impl Tracker {
    pub(crate) fn admit(self: &Arc<Self>) -> Option<TrackToken>;     // None once admission closed
    pub(crate) fn accepting(&self) -> bool;
    /// Runs `f` under the tracker's mutex, only while admission is open.
    pub(crate) fn while_accepting<R>(&self, f: impl FnOnce() -> R) -> Option<R>;
    pub(crate) fn close_admission(&self);                             // takes the same mutex
    /// Creates the zero wake-up and calls `Notified::enable()` on it, then reads the count, then
    /// waits with deadline::settle_before.
    pub(crate) async fn empty_before(&self, end: Instant) -> bool;
}
/// Not Clone; no constructor but `Tracker::admit`. Only `release` decrements the count: a token
/// dropped any other way, by an unwind included, stays counted for the rest of the process.
pub(crate) struct TrackToken { /* Arc<Tracker> */ }
impl TrackToken { pub(crate) fn release(self); }
pub(crate) struct TrackedSession { session: Session, token: TrackToken }
impl TrackedSession {
    pub(crate) fn new(session: Session, token: TrackToken) -> Self;
    /// `Session::close`; releases the token only by the release rule below.
    pub(crate) fn close(self, extra: &[ProcessIdentity], bounds: CloseBounds) -> CloseOutcome;
    /// `Session::close_by` (the stop's close, §2.3); the same release rule.
    pub(crate) fn close_by(self, extra: &[ProcessIdentity], kill_deadline: Instant, bounds: CloseBounds) -> CloseOutcome;
}
impl std::ops::Deref for TrackedSession { type Target = Session; }
pub(crate) struct TrackedChild { child: Option<std::process::Child>, token: Option<TrackToken> }
impl TrackedChild {
    /// Through `spawn_locked_before`: holding the spawn lock, takes a token from `Tracker::admit`,
    /// then runs the OS spawn outside the tracker's mutex through `host::act_before(deadline, …)`.
    /// `None`: the spawn lock not taken by the deadline, admission closed (no token), or the deadline
    /// passed after the admit (the token released); no child in each. On `Err` it releases the token.
    pub(crate) fn spawn(cmd: &mut Command, tracker: &Arc<Tracker>, deadline: Instant) -> Option<io::Result<Self>>;
    pub(crate) fn kill(&mut self);                                        // Child::kill on its handle
    /// `try_wait` on its handle; on `Some(status)` it drops the `Child` (closing its handles), then
    /// releases the token.
    pub(crate) fn try_reap(&mut self) -> Option<std::process::ExitStatus>;
}
```

**The release rule.** A token is released only where the daemon has seen the process and every handle to it gone:

- a spawn error whose `cleanup` is `Cleanup::Confirmed` (`NeverStarted`, or a `StartedThenFailed` whose teardown the backend confirmed);
- a `TrackedSession::close` or `close_by` whose `CloseOutcome.released` is true in every field (`input`, `control`, `output`, `tree_dead`) and whose `abandoned` is empty: the tree was proven dead and the session's threads joined, so the waiter that may hold the child's last handle (on Windows the job handle, pty-windows.md:172) has dropped it. On macOS this is tree death and handle release, not a confirmed reap of the leader: the child's drop makes one `waitpid(NOHANG)` and does not report it (`unix/child.rs:231-242`);
- a `TrackedChild` whose `try_wait` returned its status, after its `Child` was dropped, or whose creation made no child.

Every other end leaves the token counted: `Cleanup::Unresolved`; a close that leaves `pty-wait`, `pty-writer`, or `pty-drain` running, or does not prove the tree dead; a `TrackedSession` dropped without `close` (its `Session`'s own drop closes with `&[]`, reports nothing, and keeps a panic of that close inside the drop, so an unwind through any op that owns a session never aborts the daemon); and any unwind through `Session::spawn`, `Session::kill_by`, `Session::close`, or `Session::close_by`, since the unwind drops the token without releasing it. No `std::thread::panicking()` check is needed, because dropping is never a release. The death record is separate and follows §2.4.

Every site that takes or moves a token:

| Site | Token |
|---|---|
| spawn op, §2.3 step 4 | taken from `Tracker::admit`; moved into the `spawn_blocking` closure; inside `TrackedSession::new` on `Ok`; released on an `Err` with `Cleanup::Confirmed`; counted on any other `Err` or an unwind |
| publication, §2.3 step 5 | the `TrackedSession` moves into the slot (`ops`) inside `Tracker::while_accepting` |
| late creation (below) | stays with the op, which closes the `TrackedSession` |
| stop op, exit op, participant, late creation: closing | the `TrackedSession` moves out of the slot into the blocking task that runs the kills and the close; `TrackedSession::close` or `close_by` applies the release rule; a stop's task that never runs (the process exited first) leaves it counted |
| git probe, §2.3 step 1 | taken from `Tracker::admit` inside `TrackedChild::spawn`, under the spawn lock and before the OS spawn (none once admission closed); released there on a spawn `Err`; held in the `GitLease`; released by `try_reap`; on any other end of the probe, a panic included, the lease's drop kills the child and moves the `TrackedChild` into `GitSlot.left` |
| `GitSlot.left` | released by the tick's or the participant's `try_reap`, each of which kills first |

Nothing else creates a process (A25).

**What a counted token costs.** A token never released keeps the tracker nonzero, so every later graceful stop of that daemon process returns `LeftRunning` and exits 1 with `lock_held_at_exit` instead of 0 (daemon-lifecycle §2.8 step 6), and the OS releases the instance lock at process exit instead of the daemon releasing it before. This is the daemon process's own exit code and its log's exit reason; `chaldea daemon stop` waits for the process to be gone and still prints `stopped` and exits 0 (`daemon_cmd.rs:1112-1114`), a contract 10a1 keeps. This does not defeat the purpose: the lock rule exists so that no new daemon starts while a process the old one created may still be held or being created, each counted token is such a case, and the next daemon starts once the old process has exited. The cases are a backend cleanup it could not confirm, a close that could not prove the tree dead or left a thread running, and a panic, not a normal stop. The launch's record is separate: unconfirmed when the close did not prove the tree dead, confirmed when it did and only a thread was left running.

**Shutdown.** Before the participant runs, HTTP's drain has requested the abort of the runners still running at its deadline (`http/mod.rs:364-376`), but ops outlive their runners (§2.1 rule 2), so creations continue until admission closes. The participant gets `end` (participants share the 2 s between `C − 5 s` and `C − 3 s`, daemon-lifecycle §2.14); with `t0` its start, `r = end − t0`, `j = min(300 ms, r/4)`, `lock_by = t0 + r/4`, and `close_end = end − j`, all stored on the supervisor:

1. `Tracker::close_admission()`. The tick stops.
2. The git slot, in a task parallel to steps 3–5, until the lease is free and `left` is empty, or until `end`: each round creates the `GitSlot.changed` wake-up and calls `Notified::enable()` on it, then, on a blocking task joined with `settle_before` until `end` (a round that answers later ends the task), under the slot's mutex, reads the lease and `left`; a child in `left` is killed and `try_reap`ed through its own handle; with the lease free and `left` empty the task ends; else it waits with `settle_before` for the wake-up or 10 ms, whichever is first, bounded by `end`. A child a probe still holds is killed by that probe at its next poll, since admission is closed, and reaped by it, or handed to `left`, which wakes this task (§2.3 step 1).
3. By `lock_by`: the op lock of every published live entry (`settle_before`, `in_time()`; a lock not taken is left to its holder).
4. For each locked slot that still holds a session, in parallel: take the `TrackedSession` out, `Session::seal` (records appended to the launch's `settled`), and `TrackedSession::close` with `CloseBounds::default()` scaled by `(close_end − now) / CloseBounds::default().total()` (`limits.rs:61-78`) on a blocking task, joined with `settle_before` until `close_end`. With `r = 2 s` a close starting at `lock_by` gets 1.2 s, its kill 545 ms: the HUP and TERM waits end at 136 ms and 272 ms (pty-unix §2.5 step 3).
5. For each close whose result was received: one job, submitted with `not_after = end` and its reply awaited until `end`, by the launch's recorded state: `starting` (published, but no `launch_started` committed: its start job failed or is pending) → the start job's own records from `Session::identity` (`launch_started`, `StateInput::Spawned`, and the positional input `sending → sent` with `sent_after_seq` = that event's seq, in the same job), then as `running`; `running` → `StateInput::StopRequested`; `stopping` → nothing more; then `launch_exited { reason: stopped }` with the close's `tree_dead`, the method when confirmed, `ExitFacts.sealed` = the launch's `settled`, and the exit steps; `exited` → no job.
6. `Tracker::empty_before(end)`: empty by `end` → `ParticipantEnd::Clean`; else `ParticipantEnd::LeftRunning`. A zero first read at or after `end` is not adopted, since it cannot tell whether the count was zero by `end`.

The participant's result reads no slot and no op lock: whatever steps 2–5 did not end (a creation in flight, an op holding a lock, a session between its slot and its close, a close still running, an unreaped git child) holds a token, and so does whatever the release rule could not confirm. The publications are final once step 1 returns, so step 3 sees every published entry.

**Late creations.** An op whose `Session::spawn` returns after admission closed finds that `Tracker::while_accepting` did not run its publication: it neither publishes nor starts the session. It seals and closes it itself with bounds scaled to `close_end` and records the step 5 job for a `starting` launch by `end`. This only shortens the life of such a session; the tracker counts it either way.

**The lock rule.** The coordinator records both `Ok(ParticipantEnd::LeftRunning)` and a participant not done by `end` (`Err(Elapsed)` of `participant_bound`, `shutdown.rs:635-638`) as reasons in step 6, so the daemon exits with the instance lock held and no new daemon starts while a process the old one created may still be held or created; the result is `FailReason::LockHeldAtExit` for that stop. `std::process::exit` (`cli/src/lib.rs:108`) then ends the process without running the destructor of a `Session` a blocking task still holds: the tracker decided, not a drop. The OS then closes what the handles kept: on macOS the PTY master, which hangs up the session, and on Windows the launch's job handle, which terminates the job's members (`KILL_ON_JOB_CLOSE`, pty-windows.md:172). Neither is a death proof; such a launch is recorded at the next start (L-5).

Each launch ends in one of these classes:

| Class | Record | At the next start |
|---|---|---|
| close confirmed, commit observed | `stopped`, confirmed | nothing |
| close not confirmed, commit observed | `stopped`, unconfirmed; survivors logged | stays unconfirmed (L-1) |
| close result not received, or lock not taken | none | `daemon_restart` if `launch_started` was committed, else `unknown_after_crash`; unconfirmed (L-1, L-5) |
| commit not observed by `end` | whatever committed | as recorded, or as the row above |

**Fatal outcomes.** Before shutdown: an exit op that cannot take its lock or record within 60 s reports `StoreFatal::LaunchUnrecorded` through `BuildCtx::sink`, unless a stop sealed the session (`stop_requested`, §2.3 "Natural exit"), and so does an exit op that panics after it took the session and before its exit job answered, while admission is open; a command op that cannot record resolves `EffectOutcome::Unrecorded`, and the runner reports `ReceiptUncompleted` (§2.3). Once admission is closed, an op that cannot record ends with `ShuttingDown` and no report. Nothing else in the supervisor stops the daemon. If a stop's jobs after its seal cannot be recorded, its kill task still runs while the daemon process runs (§2.3 Stop step 3); the op then ends `Unrecorded`, and the next start records the launch (§2.5). A shutdown this starts (a `CommitUnknown` is reported as a fatal, `ctx.rs:222-232`) can end the process before the blocking pool runs the task; its token is still counted, so the participant answers `LeftRunning` and the lock stays held, while the daemon's exit reason is `store_fatal`, which takes precedence over `lock_held_at_exit` (`shutdown.rs:772-779`; L-8). The CLI verbs use `cli::client` (`discover`, `read_owner_token`, `request`, `post_json`).

impl LiveLaunch {

    pub(crate) fn observe(&self, deadline: Option<Instant>) -> LaunchObservation;
    /// Polls, until `f`, while creation began and the entry is neither published nor removed.
    pub(crate) fn await_published(&self, f: Instant);
    /// Takes the op lock by `deadline` and runs `f` inline; `f` must not block (`submit`, `modes`).
    pub(crate) async fn with_session<R>(
        &self, deadline: Instant, f: impl FnOnce(&Session) -> R,
    ) -> Result<R, SessionError>;
    /// A blocking task that, once running and before `deadline`, takes the op lock with one
    /// `try_lock`, takes `Session::screen`, releases the lock, and copies the screen with
    /// `Screen::snapshot(clock.real(deadline))`; the join is awaited with `settle_before` until
    /// `deadline`. `None` when the lock was held, there was no session, the VT failed or its
    /// lock was not taken in time, or no answer came in time: every caller only needs whether
    /// a snapshot was taken. The op lock is held only while the screen is taken, so a
    /// snapshot never carries it into its wait for the blocking pool or the VT lock, or into
    /// its copy; a stop's drain waiting on the VT lock the copy holds is bounded by
    /// `CloseBounds::drain` (pty-runtime §2.2 "Close order").
    pub(crate) async fn snapshot_before(&self, sup: &Supervisor, deadline: Instant)
        -> Option<Snapshot>;
}

- **Where 10b plugs in.** `ExitStep`s and `TickStep`s are added in `SupervisorComponents::new`, before startup step 9 calls `reconcilers`, so the restart reconciler runs them; state a step shares with the supervisor is created there and handed to the `Supervisor` that `build` creates. 10b's `IntakeProbe` (over `LiveLaunch::observe`) and its `SupervisorHooks` (the `HookAdapter`; PR 13/14 supply the `HookDecoder`s it runs) go into the `HookSeams` that `SupervisorComponents::build` (step 11) passes to `Mounts::mvp_with` when it builds the mounts. Nothing is registered after `build`.
- **Lookup by generation.** The live map is keyed by `LaunchId`; `live(thread, launch)` returns the entry only if its `thread_id` is `thread`, so a call cannot reach another thread's or a newer launch.
- **Access rule.** The map sits under a `std::sync::Mutex` held only for map operations, never across an await, a lock, or a job; `live` and the supervisor's own paths take it. Every use of a `Session` goes through `with_session`; no code keeps a `&Session` or the guard after `f` returns. `Gone`: the session left the slot (exit recorded or being recorded); `Busy`: the lock was not taken by `deadline` (`Elapsed` or `Late`, a `Late` guard dropped at once). 10b submits a unit inside `f` and awaits its completion outside it. The supervisor's own blocking calls (`kill`, `close`) are not run through `with_session`.
- **Observation.** `LiveLaunch::observe(deadline)` takes no lock: `removed` set → `Unobservable`; then `Published` set → `published_last_started(deadline)` (pty-runtime §2.3 "Published positions"; `None` → `Unobservable`), `removed` read again (set → `Unobservable`), giving `Started { reference, last_started }`; else `creation_started` set → `Unobservable` (the writer may already dispatch, for example a VT reply to the CLI's startup queries, before the session reaches the op, and a failed creation may have run); else `Reserved` (no child exists, so no write can precede the hook). `Supervisor::remove` clones the entry, releases the map, sets `removed`, then removes the entry, so a holder of the entry never sees it gone from the map with `removed` unset; `detached` is not consulted (a detached entry still in the map is `Started`). A class position above `reference` is a write of that class after the reference; a write between the creation and `reference` is before it. 10b's `IntakeProbe` holds the entry through `SendLaunch.entry`, waits for publication with `await_published` while creation began (polling the atomics, no lock), observes with its facts deadline, and encodes the observation as its `IntakeFacts` (hook-ingest §2.4); the settle observes with `None` (one attempt). `Unobservable` tells 10b the facts are missing, never that no write happened.
- **Stop before write** (SD18). `Session::seal` closes the queue: every unit not yet permitted is settled `Closed` with zero bytes, its `Completion` run before `seal` returns, and its record returned; close's own step 1 then finds the queue closed. A stop seals before its `StopRequested` job, holding the op lock, so no unit is permitted once the state is `stopping`; a unit permitted before the seal follows the PTY contract (it may still reach the backend, pty-runtime §2.3 "Write permission"). From the seal to the `StopRequested` commit the thread reads `running` and every submission is refused `Closed`.
- **Sealed results reach the exit job.** Every path that closes a live session seals it first and appends the records to the launch's `settled`: the stop at its step 3, the exit op, the participant, and a late creation before their close. Whoever records the exit passes `settled` as `ExitFacts.sealed`, immutable data in that job; a second seal adds nothing. Each zero-byte result is applied once: 10b's exit step applies a sealed record only to an input of this launch still `sending`, found by the record's `WriteOrigin::Input { input_id }`; 10b's completion consumer applies a completion only to an input still `sending` in its own job and ignores it after `launch_exited` (pty-runtime §2.3 "How the daemon uses it"). Whichever job commits first applies it; the other finds the input settled.
- **Lock holders.** A spawn op (slot insertion → start job commit), a stop op (lock → exit job commit), an exit op (lock → exit job commit), the participant, and 10b's calls, each bounded (§2.10). Order: the map mutex, then `ops`; a store job may run while `ops` is held, never the other way round.
- **Exit job order:** `launch_exited` with the reducer's `Exited`; then the registered `ExitStep`s in registration order (10b's `SendExitStep`: the sealed records and the writer outcomes it holds, its gate state, then the launch's open cards `expired`, at restart too); then 10a1's input sweep: the thread's inputs still in `queued`, `sending`, or `sent` whose `launch_id` is `L` or none (queued for the thread before the exit) move `queued → cancelled`, `sending`/`sent → unconfirmed` with `input_unconfirmed`, through `InputInput::LaunchExited`, or `InputInput::DaemonRestarted` when `restart` (mvp CL-6); `unconfirmed` inputs keep their state; then `buffer::resolve_unbindable`. The job passes the nudge as `after_ok` (it ends the launch's agent-token validity, PR 16). After commit the `after_commit` hooks run in order (10b: drop the launch's writer queue). The restart reconciler runs the same steps with `restart: true` (§2.5).

### 2.12 Handed to PR 10a2

PR 10a2's design is `docs/design/supervisor-recovery.md`.

**What moves.** The restart probe of old processes (the recorded identities of `daemon_restart` launches, including those an earlier start recorded, and of the children of failed starts whose cleanup was unresolved), launch evidence for the owner, `stop --assume-dead` and its preview, the evidence PR 21's finder (deferred) receives for A29, the confirmation of every launch 10a1 leaves unconfirmed (§2.4), and mvp P-18's survivor policy, an owner decision taken when 10a2 is designed. 10a2 implements the owner decision of 2026-11-25 (header): a check that covers only recorded identities unlocks nothing; the owner confirms after reading the evidence.

**What 10a1 records for it.** All of it is in append-only events, so launches 10a1 marked stay readable across upgrades and repeated restarts, and a later restart changes none of them (§2.5):

- the identity of every started launch in `launch_started { pid, start_time, pgid_or_job }`;
- the identity a `StartedThenFailed` returned with an unresolved cleanup, in `launch_exited.failed_start` (a launch with no `launch_started`); a `start_time` or `pgid_or_job` the backend did not have is absent, and 10a2 does not match such a record by pid alone;
- why each unconfirmed launch is unconfirmed, in `launch_exited { reason, death_confirmed: false }`: `daemon_restart` (identity recorded), `unknown_after_crash` (none recorded: A2, A29, or a late creation whose record did not commit), `stopped` or `exit` (owned kills and a close could not prove), `spawn_failed` (a `StartedThenFailed` cleanup unresolved);
- `launch_reserved`'s `recorded_at`, the thread's agent and cwd, and the `resume_blocked` raise of a refused resume.

**What 10a1 only logs.** The survivors of an owned kill or close that could not prove death, the kill and close reports of an unresolved `StartedThenFailed` cleanup, and the resolved program path. A launch 10a1 marked therefore offers 10a2 no survivor list; recording those is the evidence design of 10a2, from its first release on.

**Where 10a2 plugs in.** The `stop` intent's `death_unconfirmed` branch (§2.2); `SupervisorComponents::attach` for a prober task; `Tracker::admit` for any process a prober creates, so the participant counts it; and the daemon-lifecycle lock rule through `ParticipantEnd`.

## 3. Decisions

Decisions are numbered SD (supervisor decision), apart from the obligation ids of §4 such as D2 and D4. Each compares options by gain, loss, reversibility, and cost. "Public" marks a decision visible to users or clients.

**SD1. Nudge for revoking intents (E4 vs X1).** http-surface §2.4 says command PRs pass the revocation nudge as `after_ok` to `run_write`, but a command's intent job is the runner's (`commands.rs:665` passes `None`) and `Prepared` has no `after_ok`.

| Option | Gain | Loss | Reversibility | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. `CommandHandler::revokes() -> bool` (default false); the runner passes the nudge as `after_ok` for that handler's intent job** | one-transaction close; one line in the runner; PR 16 and the deferred PR 20 reuse it | a nudge also after a refusal or a replay (harmless: "the nudge carries no decision; the database does", http-surface §2.12) | trait default; removable | small |
| B. `Prepared` gains `after_ok` | per-call choice | `prepare` needs the nudge, which exists only after `attach` | additive | small |
| C. close always `Pending`, revoking in the op's own job | no runner change | two jobs and a second check; a crash leaves `unknown_after_crash` for a plain close | reversible | medium |
| D. rely on the socket's periodic check | none | breaks http-surface §2.4 | — | — |

**Adopted: A.** The supervisor's own revoking jobs (close `--stop`'s closing job, the exit job) pass the `RevocationNudge` themselves; the restart reconciler needs none.

**SD2. Crash windows without identity (A2, A29).** A crash between `launch_reserved` and `launch_started` leaves no identity; on Windows the A29 instance also leaves a suspended child outside the launch's job (pty-windows §2.2 "Crash window"). Adopted: record `unknown_after_crash` with death unconfirmed and keep pty-windows' suspended fallback; what the owner and PR 21's finder (deferred) are given is 10a2's (§2.12). Rejected: refusing the suspended fallback (every host or case where the job-list attempt fails, pty-windows §2.2 stage 5 "Suspended attempt", could no longer launch).

**SD3. Restart records and does not probe (A1, F1).** mvp §4 asks for a restart scan of the launch's session by recorded sid with `launch.start_time ≤ start ≤ restart_now`. Adopted: 10a1's restart records exits, recovers inputs, and revokes tokens (§2.5), and touches no old process; the probe of recorded identities is 10a2's, under the owner decision. Rejected: the sid scan (start stamps are wall-clock stamps used only for equality, pty-unix §2.1 "Identity format"; without ownership, sid `L` can name a later session once `L` is reused, so a clean scan proves nothing: pty-unix's census is sound only while the daemon owns the leader, pty-unix §2.4 "Ownership is a precondition"). Public: mvp P-18 is not met until 10a2 (L-2).

**SD4. Stop while starting (X5).** `state.rs:255-258` says the daemon holds such a stop until the spawn outcome is recorded.

| Option | Gain | Loss | Reversibility | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. The stop op waits on the launch's op lock (10 s), then decides on the recorded outcome** | matches the reducer's rule with existing parts; the slot precedes the reservation, so there is always a lock to wait on | a stop may wait for a slow spawn | — | small |
| B. A new 409 code; the client retries | no wait | every client (CLI, PWA, Rescue button) repeats the retry | additive | small |
| C. A queued stop applied by the start job | no waiting request | cross-call state | — | medium |

**Adopted: A.** A lock not taken in 10 s is 504 `launch_busy` and the stop does not happen later; a running launch's stop waits on the lock the same 10 s (SD20).

**SD5. `GET /api/adapters` wire shape (C6). Public.**

| Option | Gain | Loss | Reversibility | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. `AdaptersResponse { host_os, adapters: [AdapterInfo { agent, pty_encoding, first_prompt }] }`, this host's rows, open enums** | small; every field exists in `CAPABILITIES`; additive later | clients learn other hosts' rows only from those hosts | fields and rows can be added | small |
| B. All four rows | one static catalog everywhere | rows a client of this host cannot use; removing them later breaks clients | — | small |
| C. The whole mvp §2.7 table as fields | complete | most columns are PR 12–14's and unimplemented | — | large |
| D. A free-form map | no schema churn | no contract for clients | — | small |

**Adopted: A.** What would change it: a client that must plan launches for another OS before contacting that host.

**SD6. Who attributes `extra` (B1, mvp §4 "PR 10").** hook-ingest D10 gives the obligation to PR 10 entirely and PR 9 records none. Adopted: PR 10a1; in the MVP the list is empty (§2.4). Rejected: recording hook-emitter ancestry as `extra` (a matching creation time identifies a process, not its provenance; pty-windows §2.5 "Extras are trusted input").

**SD7. Restart reconciler order.** Adopted: receipts → supervisor launches (with inputs and tokens) → supervisor buffers (§2.5). Alternatives: inputs in a separate job after launches (a crash between them leaves exited launches with inputs still `sending`: the next restart fixes it, but one job is simpler); buffers before launches (rejected by daemon-lifecycle §2.14: the launches step decides which launches can no longer bind).

**SD8. Exit hook points for 10b.** Adopted: the ordered `ExitStep` list inside the one exit job, added in `SupervisorComponents::new`, with 10a1's input sweep after it (§2.11). Alternatives: events 10b subscribes to after commit (a gap between `launch_exited` and 10b's state, which mvp CL-6 forbids for inputs); 10b writing its own exit job (two exit records to order); steps registered on the `Supervisor` at `build` (step 11 comes after the reconcilers, so restart would miss them).

**SD9. The positional input without 10b.** Adopted: created `sending` with the reservation; `sent` with `sent_after_seq` = `launch_started`'s seq in the start job, with `reference` as its writer anchor; `rejected(spawn_failed)` when no process ran; `unconfirmed` on `StartedThenFailed`, exit, or restart. 10a1 has no ack, so a launched positional input ends `unconfirmed` at exit until 10b attributes its ack (mvp §2.3). Rejected: leaving it `sending` until 10b (mvp CL-6 forbids an input still in flight after exit).

**SD10. Ownership at shutdown. Public.** Adopted: a tracker token that every owned OS resource carries in its type from before its creation, released only by §2.9's release rule (a confirmed cleanup, a close that released everything with no thread left running, a reaped child) and otherwise counted for the rest of the process; the participant closes admission, closes the sessions it can reach against one cutoff (`end`), records what it received, and returns `LeftRunning` when the tracker is not empty by `end`, which keeps the instance lock. Rejected: a final check over slots, op locks, and in-flight calls (every hand-over between them, such as a session taken out of its slot before its close starts, is a state the check does not read, so a resource can escape it; the type leaves no such state); releasing the token whenever `close` or `spawn` returns (a waiter left running can hold the last handle to the child, on Windows the job, and an unresolved cleanup can leave a live child, so `Clean` could be reported while either is held); carrying the token into pty-runtime's waiter and teardown (it would put daemon ownership into the backend, while `CloseOutcome` and `Cleanup` already report what the conservative rule needs); leaving every session to process exit (every graceful stop would leave launches unconfirmed until 10a2, and `std::process::exit` runs no destructor of a session a blocking task holds); killing sequentially (the share is 2 s for all sessions).

**SD11. Reservation inside the op, slot first.** Adopted: both spawn and resume reserve in the op, after inserting the locked slot; a refusal or rollback removes it. Rejected: resume reserving in its intent (a stop arriving between the intent's commit and the slot's insertion would find a `starting` launch with no lock to wait on); reading `.git` files instead of running git (re-implements git's discovery, which `workspace_id`'s definition names, mvp §2.1:103).

**SD12. Session access.** Adopted: one async op lock holding `Option<TrackedSession>` per launch (§2.11), with the writer's position read through a `WriterObserver` outside it and without waiting on the intake path. Rejected: `Arc<Session>` clones (no lock wait, but `close` consumes the `Session`, so the closer would wait for every clone to drop: cross-call state and a second wait); reading positions under `ops` (an early hook would meet the spawn op's lock and see nothing); waiting on the map or queue lock in `observe` (a descheduled holder would hold the intake past its facts deadline; the positions are published for lock-free reads instead, `docs/design/stop-facts.md` D1, D2).

**SD13. Env scrub by name. Public.** mvp §2.7 lists the prefixes `CLAUDE_CODE_*` and "other `CODEX_*` session variables". Both prefixes also carry user configuration (Claude Code's settings page lists `CLAUDE_CODE_*` variables that select the provider and behaviour; `CODEX_HOME` selects Codex's home); dropping them changes how the user's CLI runs, against mvp P-1 (the CLI runs with the user's own configuration). Adopted: the named identity variables of prior-art row 6c plus `CODEX_SESSION_ID` and every `CHALDEA_*`, compared as the backend compares keys; a new identity variable is a list entry (PR 13/14 per CLI version).

**SD14. Hook configuration stays PR 13's and PR 14's.** 10a1 supplies the token, the env, and the Claude session flag; which events each CLI reports is the adapters' (mvp §4 rows 13, 14; Codex's trust hash makes the event list part of the first release, mvp §2.7). Consequence: a real CLI launched by 10a1 alone never binds, and real same-session resume is PR 13's and PR 14's acceptance (L-4).

**SD15. No spike watchdog (F2).** The emergency kill is `chaldea stop` (a Rescue button, deferred with PR 20, would call it). A watchdog killing on CPU or time would decide orchestration in daemon code (invariant 2).

**SD16. `chaldea wait`, and the CLI's reads. Public.** `chaldea wait <thread> [--until exited|idle|attention] [--timeout <dur>]` (default `exited`, 10 min; a `<dur>` too long for a monotonic instant from now is a usage error, checked when the argument is parsed and again when the cutoff is built). One cutoff, taken at start, is passed to `poll_until_with` and to every request's connect and read (`client::request` takes it as `deadline`). Each poll reads `GET /api/threads/{thread_id}` (`ThreadSummary`, §2.8). The conditions: `exited` holds when `process_state` is `exited`; `idle` when `process_state` is `running` and `turn_state` is `idle`; `attention` when `attention_count > 0`. A condition that holds → print the summary as JSON, exit 0 (level-triggered: a condition already true returns at once). A wait for `idle` or `attention` that finds `process_state` `exited` without its condition → exit 1, "the launch exited". Discovery `NotRunning`, `Unknown`, `Starting`, or `Stopping`, a refused connection, a reset or EOF before a full response, 408, or 503 → keep polling; 404 → exit 1, "no such thread or not readable"; 401 or 403 → exit 1; any other status or a body that does not decode → exit 1 with the reason; past the cutoff, including a request that ended `TimedOut` at it → exit 4 (`ExitCode::Timeout`; 3 is `AlreadyRunning`). A response read after the cutoff is not adopted; discovery reads files and looks up a process synchronously and cannot be cut short, so the exit follows the cutoff by at most that one read. `chaldea resume` and `chaldea close` read the same route once, send its current launch as `expected_launch_id` with a new `command_id`, keep both on every transport retry of that request, and exit 1 on `launch_changed` without sending again; on `death_unconfirmed` they print §2.2's message. Rejected: the thread snapshot (`GET /api/events` with a snapshot) as the CLI's read (its size grows with the thread's inputs and attention and can exceed the CLI's 1 MiB bound); an edge-triggered wait on the event stream (`--after <seq>`), left for 10b's `send` then `wait` pattern.

**SD17. Launch operations as supervisor tasks.** Adopted: rule 2 of §2.1, with `EffectOutcome` telling the runner how an op ended (§2.3). Rejected: running spawn, stop, and close inside the runner's effect future (an abort at the drain deadline would drop the future while its blocking call runs on, leaving a `Session` or a close result with no owner); leaving the effect pending when a job cannot be recorded (the runner and the HTTP drain would wait for it until their deadlines); one value for both an unrecordable job and a shutdown (a graceful stop would report a fatal and end as `StoreFatal`); answering a shutdown-ended op with `shutting_down` (that code says nothing ran, while the intent and earlier jobs have committed, and a client retrying with a new `command_id` could repeat the work).

**SD18. Stop before write.** Adopted: `Session::seal` before the `StopRequested` job, its zero-byte records held on the launch and passed to the exit job (§2.11). Rejected: committing `StopRequested` and relying on close's step 1 (a unit whose recheck passed could be permitted after the state became `stopping`); a supervisor flag read by 10b's recheck (the recheck runs outside the queue lock, before the dispatch boundary, pty-runtime §2.3); relying on the completion consumer alone (the exit job can commit before the consumer's job, and the sweep would make a zero-byte input `unconfirmed`).

**SD19. A failed start's identity in `launch_exited`. Public.** When `StartedThenFailed` returns an identity and its cleanup is unresolved, 10a1 holds a fact 10a2 needs and no later release can recover from the store. Adopted: the optional `launch_exited.failed_start { pid, start_time?, pgid_or_job? }` (additive, no `schema_version` change, mvp §2.1 "`schema_version`"), each optional part absent when the backend returned it empty; the projection is unchanged and 10a2 reads the event. The field carries `#[serde(default, skip_serializing_if = "Option::is_none")]` and `#[ts(optional)]` (as `http.rs:96` does), a stated exception to `payload.rs:3-4`'s rule that an `Option` is always present as `null`: an absent key keeps every existing `launch_exited` payload and golden fixture (`crates/core/tests/golden.rs`) unchanged, and `schema_agreement.rs` checks both forms. Rejected: a `launch_started` for such a launch (it would claim a start transition the backend reports as failed, and the reducer would see a `Spawned` before the exit); logging only (a launch marked before 10a2 would lose the identity with the log).

**SD20. The stop's deadline and cleanup budget. Public.** mvp P-3 asks that no process of the tree be alive 5 s after `chaldea stop`, and mvp §4 PR 10 that the supervisor retry through `Session::kill` with the full list while it owns the session, then close explicitly. No option guarantees a signal or a proof by an instant: a blocking task, the store, or the whole runtime can be paused past it.

| Option | Gain | Loss | Reversibility | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. ## 6. Tests

**Per claim**: §5.2's test column. Unit tests drive `FakePty` (`FakeScript`, `FakeChild::exit`, `FakeChild::fail_wait`, `FakeChild::hold_kill_lock`, `FakeScript.hold_waiter`, `KillBehavior::Unconfirmed`, `KillBehavior::Panic`, `FakeEvent::Kill`, `FakeEvent::Signal`, `FakePty::spawns`) and the store directly, with `cfg(test)` pause points of the supervisor; integration tests start the store and `HttpServer` in-process with the native runtime and FakeCli, as `hook_e2e.rs` does; the restart and E6 tests run the daemon in a helper process (the test binary re-executes itself, daemon-lifecycle §7 "Helper-process tests") with `Seams.components` holding a pause, and kill it by PID. No release-binary variable or flag enables a pause.

**FakePty additions** (`fake.rs`): `FakeEvent::Kill` records every `kill_tree` call at its entry, and the new `FakeEvent::Signal` records the fake's signal-equivalent operation, which it performs exactly when a native backend would signal: the call is not expired, it holds the lock (free at its first try, or freed before `lock_by`), the child is alive, and its behaviour is not `Unconfirmed`. An expired call performs no operation and reports `NotAttempted`, as the native backends' expired calls make no OS call; a call that finds the child already dead reports `AlreadyDead` with no `FakeEvent::Signal`. Each report carries `attempt` as the native backends set it, and `proved_at` is the real instant at which the fake proved the tree dead. `FakeChild::hold_kill_lock` returns a guard; while it is held, a call waits for it until `lock_by`, and a lock released at or after `lock_by` counts as not taken, as §2.3 Stop step 4 defines for the native backends: `method: "fake_busy"`, `attempt: NotAttempted`, `tree_dead: false`, and no `FakeEvent::Signal`. `KillBehavior::Unconfirmed` returns at once with no `FakeEvent::Signal`, `attempt: NotAttempted`, `tree_dead: false`, and a method that is not busy, as `unix_ownership_lost` does; `KillBehavior::ProveOnCall(n)` makes the calls before the `n`-th signal and return at once with `attempt: Signalled`, `tree_dead: false`, and a method that is not busy, and the `n`-th prove the tree dead; `KillBehavior::Panic` panics inside `kill_tree`; `FakeScript.hold_waiter` keeps the fake child's `wait_timeout` blocked until the test releases it, so a close returns with `pty-wait` in `abandoned`.

**Locating FakeCli.** `supervisor_e2e.rs` lives in `crates/cli/tests` because its hooks need the `chaldea` emitter (`CARGO_BIN_EXE_chaldea`), and it finds `chaldea-fake-cli` as `hook_e2e.rs` does (`fake_cli()`, built by `cargo test --workspace`). pty-unix §2.8 calls that lookup a fallback; the alternative, a thin `[[bin]]` in the `cli` package, would ship a test binary with the CLI.
